Calendars and Attendance

When students attend school every day, they build strong habits, relationships, and learning momentum. Missing just a few days each month can make it harder to keep up. Use the resources below to plan ahead and help your student stay on track all year.

Every Day Counts. Plan ahead. 

Good attendance starts with planning. Here are a few ways to make it easier:

  • Review your school’s calendar early to plan vacations or appointments.
  • Set morning and bedtime routines for smoother starts.
  • Keep contact information updated so your school can reach you quickly.
  • Communicate with teachers or counselors if you notice attendance challenges.

Did you know? Missing just two days a month—whether excused or unexcused—adds up to more than two weeks of lost learning each year.

Tips to Support Attendance at Every Stage

  • Pre-K & kindergarten: Build the habit early
  • Elementary: Make every day count
  • Middle & high school: Stay engaged and involved

Pre-K & kindergarten: Build the habit early

There is a common misconception that daily attendance in Pre-K and kindergarten isn’t necessary. This is not the case. In addition to the critical foundational learning that takes place, Pre-K and kindergarten builds strong routines and helps students feel comfortable, confident, and connected at school. Consistent attendance at this early stage lays the foundation for reading skills, classroom habits, and long-term academic success. Here are some tips:

  • Visit your child’s school together before the year starts.
  • Set consistent bedtimes and morning routines a few weeks ahead of school.
  • Help your child get excited with books and stories about school.
  • Build a backup plan for getting to school when transportation is tricky.

Elementary: Make every day count

  • Talk about why going to school each day matters.
  •  Schedule doctor’s appointments and family trips outside of school days when possible.
  •  Connect with your child’s teacher if your student seems nervous or anxious.
  •  If your child is sick, ask the teacher for ideas to keep up learning at home.
     

Middle & high school: Stay engaged and involved

  • Check in regularly with your student to understand their feelings toward school.
  • Encourage healthy routines, including sleep and homework habits.
  • Watch for signs of stress, bullying, or discipline issues, and reach out to school staff if you have concerns. 
  • Get familiar with attendance policies and review your child’s attendance record often.

Reporting Absences 

If your student must miss school, please send a written or digital note within two days of returning.

Excused absences include:

  • Illness or injury

  • A death in the family

  • Health care appointments

  • Court appearances

  • Religious observances

  • Natural disasters

  • Educational opportunities  (requires advance approval)

To request an excused absence for an educational reason, complete one of these forms before the absence:

  • Submit educational absence request 
  • Submit educational absence request (Spanish) 

Without a note, the absence will be marked unexcused. If your child has three or more unexcused absences, your school will contact you to discuss ways to support regular attendance.

Reminder: Students are responsible for completing all missed work. Teachers will share schedules and expectations for making up assignments.

For details, read School Board Policy 4400: Attendance.
